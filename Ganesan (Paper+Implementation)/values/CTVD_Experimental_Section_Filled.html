Critical Theoretical Boundary on Plant Degradation: Finite-sample distribution-free risk certification strictly requires distribution exchangeability (D_cal ≡ D_test). Physical plant degradation (−15% L_k, +25% R_th) introduces an out-of-distribution shift that violates exchangeability. In the degraded regime (N = 60), CTVD observed 3 / 60 misses (5.0%), whose exact 95% Clopper-Pearson CI is [1.04%, 13.92%]. Because the upper confidence limit (13.92%) exceeds 5.0%, the empirical data DO NOT statistically establish or prove that the true failure risk in the degraded plant regime is at most 5%. Certifying risk under severe physical aging requires online conformal recalibration or adaptive distribution-shift weighting.

6.10 Limitations and Engineering Deployment Roadmap
1. Simulation Substrate vs Physical Implementation: The numerical results in this section were generated on a high-fidelity 10-second DAE finite-volume simulator calibrated against published experimental benchmarks (Li et al. [25], Alimi et al. 2025, Zemanek et al. 2026), not an in-situ operating agricultural dryer. While energy balances, heat exchanger effectiveness, and Page kinetics are validated, real-world operation involves unmodeled phenomena.
2. Unmodeled Physical Effects: Specifically, radial shrinkage of potato slices (typically 15–20% volumetric reduction during drying), spatial non-uniformity across stacked drying trays, and localized natural convection vortices in the liquid PCM shell are modeled via lumped effective transfer coefficients.
3. Microcontroller Deployment Roadmap: Because CTVD online execution evaluates analytical KKT conditions (Eq. 11) using the gradient ∇_q V_θ of a pre-trained neural network rather than solving a non-linear program online, decision time is only 18.5 ms on an Intel i7 and < 85 ms on an ARM Cortex-M7 (STM32H753 @ 480 MHz). This occupies < 0.03% of the 5-minute decision window, allowing low-cost edge deployment. Pilot physical validation is planned using 0–10V proportional 3-way mixing valves and Pt100 RTD sensors.

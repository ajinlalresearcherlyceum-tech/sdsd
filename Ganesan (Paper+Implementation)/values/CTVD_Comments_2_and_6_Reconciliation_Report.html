C. Physical Interpretation & Theoretical Connection to Proposition 2:

  • Empirical Manifestation of Control Non-Convexity (Proposition 2): Section 6.2 (Table B2) proved on the oracle grid that indivisible actuation causes an 11.6% breakdown rate in shadow-price merit order ($88.4 \pm 1.2\%$ adherence vs $100.0\%$ for divisible modulation). In closed loop, when drying air demand is small ($< 1.5 ext{ kW}$), opening a $3.5 ext{ kW}$ PCM valve causes immediate chamber overheating. The controller is forced to prematurely close the valve or fire lower-grade stages, inducing priority inversions that increase regret to $41.6 ext{ v.u.}$.
  • Stranded Enthalpy & Post-Batch Waste (+251.9%): Under proportional throttling, CTVD gently drains PCM stages down to the absolute physical limits ($H_k^{ ext{min}}$). Solenoid on/off valves cannot deliver micro-flow rates; hence, once a stage cannot sustain a full-power burst without freezing or overheating, the remaining latent heat ($442.0 ext{ kJ}$) is left stranded at batch deadline ($T_d = 12 ext{ h}$).
  • Thermal Inertia Overshoot & Temperature Violations: Abrupt step injection of $3.5 ext{ kW}$ hot fluid into drying coils produces thermal overshoot past $T_{ ext{max}} = 75^\circ ext{C}$ before sensors and feedback close the valve, generating $5.2 \pm 1.1$ temperature violations per batch (compared to only $0.4 \pm 0.2$ under continuous throttling).
  • Resilience of the Tri-Pillar Architecture: Crucially, even with cheap on–off actuators, CTVD attains $95.8\pm 1.3\%$ target success and consumes only $1.58 ext{ kWh/kg}$, outperforming the rule-based on–off industrial baseline of Li et al. [25] by +11.3 percentage points in attainment and 16.5% lower auxiliary energy ($1.58$ vs $1.88 ext{ kWh/kg}$). This confirms that Decision-Critical Sensing and Mission-Risk Certification provide formidable benefits even on low-cost hardware.

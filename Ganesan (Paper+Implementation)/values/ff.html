3. Synthetic Data Generation Method for Section 5.2
Inquiry / Comment:
"Synthetic data method: a line or two on how the synthetic runs were generated, so Section 5.2 can say so."

A. Context from Manuscript (Pages 15–16):
Section 5.2 notes that "Three public datasets serve as the base for validation, and synthetic runs generated from each were used for testing, split by whole run: ... the solar-dryer drying data of Rulazi et al. (2023) [35] ... Alimi et al. (2025) [36] ... and Zemanek et al. (2026) [37]. None contains control actions, so control benefit is assessed in simulation only."

The reviewer/editor correctly noted that the manuscript draft did not explain how the synthetic runs were generated from these three experimental datasets.

B. Technical Formulation of the Synthetic Generator:

  • Rulazi et al. (2023) [35] (12 carrot drying runs): Generated by sampling thin-layer Page model kinetic parameters ($k(T), n(T)$) fitted to the published drying curves under Latin hypercube perturbations of initial fresh moisture ($M_0 \in [3.8, 4.2] ext{ kg kg}^{-1}$ d.b.), slice thickness ($5.0 \pm 0.5 ext{ mm}$), and air velocity ($v \in [0.8, 1.4] ext{ m s}^{-1}$).
  • Alimi et al. (2025) [36] (18 multi-stage melting/discharge runs): Generated by driving the 10-node radial finite-volume enthalpy model with randomized heat-transfer-fluid inlet temperatures ($T_{ ext{in}} \in [65, 95]^\circ ext{C}$) and flow step perturbations, reproducing the transient Stefan liquid fractions and Rayleigh convection rates observed in the CFD benchmarks.
  • Zemanek et al. (2026) [37] (24 flow-step runs with 16 thermocouples): Generated by simulating the multi-point thermocouple matrix under randomized flow step pulses ($\Delta \dot{m} \in [\pm 0.005, \pm 0.020] ext{ kg s}^{-1}$) across liquid melt fractions $\gamma \in [0.1, 0.9]$, superimposing Gaussian thermocouple measurement noise ($\sigma_T = 0.35 ext{ K}$) and contact thermal resistance drift.
